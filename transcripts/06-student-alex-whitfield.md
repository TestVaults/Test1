# Interview: Alex Whitfield (Student, Nursing, Graduate Year 1)

**Interviewer:** I understand you use a screen reader day-to-day. Can you tell me about your experience navigating Compass with it?

**Alex:** It's been a struggle, to be direct. The course search page has filter checkboxes that JAWS doesn't announce properly — it just reads "checkbox, checkbox, checkbox" with no labels, so I can't tell what I'm actually filtering by.

**Interviewer:** How do you work around that currently?

**Alex:** I ask a sighted classmate to screen-share and tell me what's on the page. That's not sustainable and honestly it's frustrating to depend on that for something as basic as registering for class.

**Interviewer:** Have you reported this to anyone?

**Alex:** I filed a ticket with disability services in February. They escalated it to IT. As of today it's still not fixed, though I did get an email last month saying it's "in the accessibility remediation backlog."

**Interviewer:** Are there parts of Compass that work well for you?

**Alex:** The clinical rotation scheduling module, surprisingly, is well built — proper labels, logical tab order, works cleanly with my screen reader. It seems like a different team built that part.

**Interviewer:** That's a helpful contrast. Anything else affecting your experience?

**Alex:** The financial aid document upload uses a drag-and-drop widget with no keyboard alternative. I literally cannot upload a document without help. That one feels urgent to me, more than the filters even.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you'd want the product team to understand above all else?

**Alex:** That inconsistency is the real problem. Some parts of Compass were clearly built with accessibility in mind and some weren't, and I never know which I'm going to hit.
