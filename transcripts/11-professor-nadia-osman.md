# Interview: Dr. Nadia Osman (Professor, Psychology)

**Interviewer:** I know you've had some concerns about Compass that you flagged to the provost's office. Can you walk me through those?

**Nadia:** Yes, mainly around data visibility. As a faculty advisor, I can see a shared advising notes panel for my advisees, which is useful, but I noticed I can also see notes left by their previous advisors going back years, including some fairly sensitive personal disclosures — mental health accommodations, family circumstances, things students shared in confidence at the time.

**Interviewer:** Do you think that visibility is a design oversight?

**Nadia:** I genuinely don't know if it's intentional. It might be reasonable for continuity of care in some cases, but there's no granularity — it's all-or-nothing. A student can't mark a note as sensitive or restrict it to a specific advisor.

**Interviewer:** Have you changed your own behavior because of this?

**Nadia:** Yes. I've stopped writing anything sensitive in the notes at all, which somewhat defeats the purpose of the feature. I now keep separate private notes outside Compass for anything delicate, which is exactly the fragmented system this was supposed to replace.

**Interviewer:** Have students raised this with you directly?

**Nadia:** One did, actually. She was uncomfortable that a new advisor could see notes from a difficult period in her freshman year without any context on why they were written. She felt like she couldn't control her own narrative.

**Interviewer:** Setting the privacy issue aside, how do you feel about Compass otherwise?

**Nadia:** Functionally it's fine — scheduling, grading, all serviceable. But I'd trade some convenience for better data governance. This feels like something that should be addressed before it becomes a bigger trust issue.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything specific you'd recommend?

**Nadia:** Granular permissions on notes, at minimum. And ideally, notify students when a new person gains access to their advising history.
