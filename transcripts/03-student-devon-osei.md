# Interview: Devon Osei (Student, History, PhD Candidate)

**Interviewer:** As a grad student your relationship to registration is a bit different. What's Compass been like for you?

**Devon:** It's a mixed bag. I don't register for many courses anymore, mostly independent studies and dissertation credits, so the parts of Compass I actually use are the advising notes and the funding/stipend tracker.

**Interviewer:** How's the advising notes feature working for you?

**Devon:** That's genuinely great. My advisor can leave notes after our meetings that I can see immediately, with action items. Before Compass, that all lived in her email and sometimes things got lost. Now there's a paper trail.

**Interviewer:** Any downsides to that feature?

**Devon:** Only that not all faculty use it consistently. My advisor is diligent, but a committee member never logs meeting notes, so half my committee interactions are tracked and half aren't.

**Interviewer:** What about the stipend tracker?

**Devon:** It's supposed to show disbursement dates but it's often out of sync with what Financial Services actually does. I've seen it say "Paid" three days before the deposit actually hits.

**Interviewer:** Has that caused problems?

**Devon:** Mostly just anxiety, checking my bank account for money that isn't there yet. Nothing catastrophic, but it undermines trust in the tool.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want to flag?

**Devon:** Room booking for the grad lounge is now through Compass too, and that part is actually excellent. Real-time availability, no more sign-up sheets taped to the door.
