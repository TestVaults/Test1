# Interview: Oscar Bell (Librarian, Access Services)

**Interviewer:** How does Compass factor into library operations?

**Oscar:** Two main ways — study room bookings and course reserves. Both moved from our old standalone library system into Compass this year as part of the "unified resource booking" rollout.

**Interviewer:** How has that migration gone?

**Oscar:** Study room bookings, genuinely great. Students book directly through Compass now instead of a separate library-only tool, and no-show rates actually dropped, probably because it's tied to their main account and they get the same reminder notifications they get for classes.

**Interviewer:** What about course reserves?

**Oscar:** That's been rockier. Faculty are supposed to submit reading lists for reserves through Compass, but the form doesn't clearly explain the difference between "physical reserve" and "digital reserve," and we've gotten a lot of requests that are miscategorized. I end up calling faculty to clarify, which is exactly the manual back-and-forth this was supposed to eliminate.

**Interviewer:** Do you have a sense of how often that happens?

**Oscar:** Maybe one in five reserve requests needs a follow-up call or email. Before Compass, using our old dedicated reserves system, it was closer to one in twenty, because that system's form was more specific to our workflow.

**Interviewer:** Any positives specific to your role?

**Oscar:** Inventory visibility. I can now see in real time which reserve items are checked out and by whom, cross-referenced with the class roster, which helps me chase down overdue items much faster than before.

**Interviewer:** What would you change first?

**Oscar:** Rebuild the reserves submission form with library-specific language and maybe a short inline example, instead of a generic booking form repurposed for our use case.
