# can you tell me the insights from the interviews found in this folder?

These 15 transcripts are all interviews about **Compass**, a university's unified student-information/registration platform, gathered from students, professors, an advisor, a librarian, a department administrator, and IT support. Several clear themes emerge:

**What's working well**
- The unified data model is a real architectural improvement over the old patchwork of systems — updates (e.g., room changes) now propagate instantly everywhere instead of syncing overnight.[^1]
- Room/space booking (classrooms, studio/kiln time, grad lounge, library study rooms) is consistently praised as fast, visual, and far better than the old spreadsheets/sign-up sheets it replaced.[^2]
- The degree audit tool and shared advising notes have measurably cut advising workload and improved handoffs between advisors.[^3]
- Course search filtering, financial aid tracking, and self-service supply/portfolio submissions are also called out positively.[^4]

**Recurring pain points**
- Session/login stability: users get logged out mid-task and lose work, especially during long form entry (grades, budget requests, complex schedules). IT support says this is close to a third of all Compass tickets and suspects an aggressive timeout that only resets on clicks, not typing.[^5]
- Registration-week overload: the system crashes or times out under peak load right when registration windows open, causing lost cart contents and missed seats.[^6]
- Confusing notifications and navigation labeling: vague status messages ("status changed") and dashboard tile labels that don't match what users search for (e.g., schedule filed under "Enrollment") drive a large share of help-desk tickets.[^7]
- Mobile experience is weak — it's a shrunk-down desktop site, not a true responsive/native app, and key actions (like approving requests) can be unusable on a phone.[^8]
- Accessibility is inconsistent: some modules (clinical rotation scheduling) work great with screen readers, while others (course search filters, financial aid drag-and-drop upload) are effectively unusable without sighted help.[^9]
- Workflow rigidity in specific modules: budget approval enforces a strict linear approver sequence that blocks parallel sign-off; recurring room bookings can't have a single occurrence cancelled; grade entry and grade submission were split into separate steps without clear guidance, causing missed grade postings.[^10]
- A significant data-governance concern was raised by a psychology professor: advising notes are visible all-or-nothing across advisor history, exposing sensitive past disclosures (mental health, family circumstances) to new advisors with no granular permissions, which has caused at least one advisor to stop logging sensitive notes in the system at all.[^11]
- Other gaps: opaque transfer-credit review status, a library reserves form that doesn't distinguish physical vs. digital reserves, a stipend tracker that's out of sync with actual disbursements, no bulk actions for advisors handling hundreds of students, slow record loading at high usage volumes, and no API/export for institutional data analysis.[^12]

Overall, the interviews paint Compass as a genuine architectural upgrade whose core data model and several flagship features (advising notes, room booking, degree audits) are well liked, but whose reliability under load, mobile/accessibility support, notification clarity, and a few rigid or under-designed workflows are the main sources of frustration across nearly every user group.
