# that's perfect can you tell me the specific differences between each user type? Can you tell me their needs, pain points, goals?

Here's a breakdown by user type, with the differences that show up between individuals of the same type:

**STUDENTS** (Jordan-Sophomore CS, Maria-Senior Biology, Devon-PhD History, Priya-Freshman Business/first-gen, Callum-Junior Art transfer, Alex-Grad Nursing/screen-reader user)
- Needs vary sharply by year: freshmen need onboarding/wayfinding, seniors need reliable high-stakes registration to graduate on time, grad students barely register for courses and instead live in advising notes and funding tools, transfers need credit-evaluation transparency.[^1]
- Goals: Jordan wants efficient course search and clear status updates; Maria's goal is simply to lock in required seats before they fill; Devon wants a reliable paper trail with advisors and accurate stipend info; Priya wants to understand the system well enough to find basic things like her schedule and financial aid appeal; Callum wants visibility into his transfer-credit timeline; Alex's goal is to register and submit documents independently, without needing sighted help.
- Pain points differ by circumstance: Jordan gets cryptic waitlist notifications; Maria loses cart contents and class seats to registration-week crashes and unflagged lab time conflicts; Devon is bothered by inconsistent advisor note-taking and a stipend tracker that shows "Paid" before money actually arrives; Priya struggles purely with navigation/labeling (dashboard tiles that don't match what she's looking for); Callum's transfer credit review is a black box with no timeline; Alex faces real accessibility barriers — unlabeled filter checkboxes and a drag-and-drop upload with no keyboard alternative — that block him from completing tasks independently.[^2]
- Shared thread: most students value features that save time (course search filters, degree audit, room booking) but are most frustrated when Compass fails at high-stakes moments (registration, deadlines) or hides status behind unclear labels/notifications.

**PROFESSORS** (Harold-Chemistry/27yrs, Elena-English/11yrs, Simon-CS/6yrs, Lila-Economics/9yrs, Nadia-Psychology/14yrs)
- Needs differ by how they use Compass: Harold mainly needs to submit grades quickly; Elena needs shared advising notes and mobile-friendly approvals; Simon cares about backend reliability and data export for research; Lila needs fast, low-friction room booking for seminars; Nadia needs safe, granular handling of sensitive advising data.[^3]
- Goals: Harold just wants his pre-Compass one-page grade workflow back; Elena wants smoother advisor handoffs and to approve requests from her phone; Simon wants to trust the system's session stability and get his own data out via API/export; Lila wants to keep spending minutes, not a day, on room logistics; Nadia wants to keep using shared notes without compromising student privacy.
- Pain points are quite distinct per person: Harold was blindsided by a hidden two-step grade certification process; Elena's mobile approval button is literally cut off-screen; Simon gets randomly logged out mid-task, losing complex work like a syllabus schedule; Lila can't cancel a single occurrence of a recurring room booking without canceling the whole series; Nadia discovered she can see sensitive notes written by prior advisors with no granularity or restriction, which is a data-governance/trust concern rather than a workflow annoyance.[^4]
- Contrast: Harold is broadly "adversarial" toward Compass while Lila and Elena are largely positive — seniority/tenure and department size don't predict sentiment; it tracks more with which specific modules they touch daily.[^5]

**ACADEMIC ADVISOR (Teresa Nakagawa)** — advises ~300 students across three departments
- Needs: speed at scale (fast record loads across hundreds of students) and cross-departmental visibility.[^6]
- Goals: minimize per-student overhead during back-to-back appointments; get through advising season efficiently.
- Pain points: student record pages take 8-10 seconds to load; no bulk actions exist, so routine tasks like logging the same note for 15 students must be repeated one at a time; no dashboard-level "flag for follow-up" status.[^7]
- Distinct from professors/students: her needs are about volume and throughput (hundreds of interactions), not individual-transaction correctness.

**LIBRARIAN (Oscar Bell)** — Access Services
- Needs: a resource-booking system tailored to library-specific workflows (study rooms, course reserves) and real-time inventory visibility.
- Goals: reduce no-shows and manual back-and-forth with faculty submitting reserves.
- Pain points: the course-reserves form is a generic booking form that doesn't distinguish "physical" vs. "digital" reserve, causing miscategorized requests roughly 1-in-5 times (worse than the old dedicated system's 1-in-20).[^8]
- Distinct from other roles: his complaint is about a form being too generic/unspecialized for his workflow, not about performance or accessibility.

**DEPARTMENT ADMINISTRATOR (Francine Doyle)** — Mechanical Engineering, manages 22 faculty
- Needs: department-wide scheduling conflict detection and a budget-approval workflow that matches how her department actually operates (parallel, not strictly sequential approvals).
- Goals: cut down the week-long process of building a 40-section master schedule; keep routine purchases moving even when the department chair is traveling.
- Pain points: rigid linear budget-approval chain blocks the associate chair from acting when the chair hasn't yet, causing regular delays on even routine requests.[^9]
- Distinct from others: her frustration is with process/workflow rigidity (approval sequencing) rather than technical bugs, notifications, or accessibility.

**IT SUPPORT (Marcus Webb)** — Help Desk, campus-wide view
- Needs: not a personal user of Compass so much as the person absorbing everyone else's pain — needs the system's session handling and labeling fixed to reduce ticket volume.
- Goals: reduce repetitive support tickets, particularly the two dominant categories he sees.
- Pain points (on behalf of the userbase): login/session timeouts account for about a third of tickets (worse for faculty on long forms), and navigation/labeling confusion is the second-largest category, especially for new students and less tech-comfortable faculty.[^10]
- Distinct from all other roles: he's an aggregator/synthesizer of everyone else's pain points rather than someone with his own individual task-based needs in Compass.

**Cross-cutting differences between types:** Students care most about registration reliability and clarity; professors split between simple task completion (grades, booking) and technical trust (session stability, data governance); staff roles (advisor, librarian, administrator, IT) care most about scale, workflow fit, and systemic patterns across many users rather than any single transaction.
