# what can you find about canadian insurance

The vault has a detailed report specifically on Canadian insurance: **canadian_insurance_competitive_intelligence_2025.md**, a competitive intelligence briefing (dated May 2025, prepared for RBC Insurance) titled 'Canadian Insurance Distribution Transformation.'[^1] Its core thesis is that the Canadian insurance distribution landscape is being reshaped by vertical integration — major carriers are acquiring the MGA (Managing General Agency) and independent advisor channels rather than just manufacturing products.[^2]

Key points from the report:
- **Desjardins** has been the most aggressive acquirer, spending ~$2.4B+ in three years, including IDC Worldsource/Worldsource Financial ($750M, 2023), Insurance Company of PEI (2024), and taking Guardian Capital fully private ($1.67B, 2026).[^3]
- **iA Financial Group** pursued PPI Management (2019, the deal that 'triggered the MGA arms race'), Vericity (US digital life insurance), and Richardson Wealth ($597M, 2025), building a ~$330B AUM/AUA integrated distribution platform.[^4]
- **Canada Life (Great-West Lifeco)** acquired Investment Planning Counsel for $575M (2023) after Financial Horizons Group (~2016), building toward 4,000+ advisor relationships and $85B+ wealth AUA.[^5]
- **Sun Life** took a different path — exiting Sponsored Markets distribution, standardizing MGA advisors onto the APEXA platform, and building a health ecosystem (Dialogue telemedicine acquisition for $277M, Lumino Health).[^6]
- **Manulife** invested $2B in digital/AI transformation (2018–2025) rather than MGA acquisitions, positioning ChatMFC AI and targeting the underinsured 'mass market' via multi-level marketing (World Financial Group).[^7]
- **BMO Insurance** launched Rovr AI, described as Canada's first AI-powered field underwriting assistant, leaning into its bancassurance advantage.[^8]
- **Empire Life** chose to stay a genuinely independent manufacturer rather than compete in the MGA acquisition race.[^9]

The report frames five cross-cutting themes: enclosure of the independent advisor channel, convergence of wealth and insurance, AI/digital becoming table stakes, growth from group benefits/government programs (like the Canadian Dental Care Plan), and a 2030 outlook where 3-4 vertically integrated platforms dominate.[^10] It also lays out threats and whitespace opportunities specifically for RBC Insurance.[^11]

Separately, a broader (not Canada-specific) file, **compass_artifact_wf-61e5e5f3-6908-4c44-883e-d66302a88af3_text_markdown.md**, on 'How AI is rewiring insurance distribution' has one Canadian mention: MyChoice (Canada) launched an AI/ChatGPT-based insurance comparison app around Feb 9, 2026, alongside similar US launches (Insurify, Experian).[^12]
